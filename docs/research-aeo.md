# Folio: AEO competitor research and landing-page claims

Research date: 13 September 2026. Scope: three direct AEO competitors, using current first-party product pages. This is a positioning comparison, not a hands-on product test or a complete market inventory. Vendor statements describe what vendors advertise; they do not prove performance. An undocumented feature is not evidence that a competitor lacks it.

## The defensible product direction

Folio's current build supports deterministic saved page audits, evidence checks, and reviewable downloadable repairs. Its AI visibility charts and rankings are sample data; its Agents API adapter is prepared, but an independent live evaluation runner is not yet implemented. These implementation facts are the basis for the present-tense claims below, not findings about competitors.

The opportunity is an inspectable engineering workflow: preserve what was checked, show the evidence for a finding, give the team a concrete repair to review, and eventually evaluate the repaired page against a separately controlled benchmark. The strength is the complete, understandable workflow. None of this research establishes an exclusive feature or market-wide first.

The proposed public benchmark should measure **technical discoverability and answerability on a stated test suite**, while separately reporting observed AI-search mentions when live monitoring is available. A page-readiness score cannot establish search ranking, recommendation frequency, revenue impact, or why a commercial answer engine omitted a brand.

## Landing-page copy safe for the current build

**Section heading: A clearer path from evidence to improvement.**

**1. See what the score is built on.**
“Open the checks behind a saved page audit and inspect the evidence for each finding.”

**2. Take a repair your team can review.**
“Review suggested changes and download a repair artifact to bring into your publishing workflow.”

**3. Know what you are looking at.**
“Explore page audit results alongside clearly labeled sample visibility and benchmark views.”

These are affirmative product statements. They should not become comparison claims such as “others hide the evidence,” “the only tool that fixes pages,” or “real rankings across every AI engine.” The third statement works best as a short product-status note beside sample charts rather than as the principal selling point.

## Roadmap copy: label before use

**Planned: A benchmark you can inspect.**
“We are building independent reader evaluations with versioned test suites, saved evidence, and public technical rankings you can inspect.”

**Planned: Measure the effect of a repair.**
“Compare a page before and after a change using a frozen evaluation suite, with the same reader configuration and scoring rules.”

Before promoting either as live, implement the runner and publish the test-suite version, page snapshot/time, model/provider version, repetitions, scoring rubric, failed or skipped cases, sample sizes, and rerun policy. Keep repair generation separate from grading and retain held-out tasks. Where evaluation outputs vary, show uncertainty rather than presenting a single precise rank as ground truth. These are proposed Folio design choices, not claims that competitors fail to do them.

## Claims to avoid

- “Different from every other AEO/SEO tool,” “first,” “only,” or “most accurate” without broader comparative evidence.
- Guaranteed citations, guaranteed SEO gains, or a page score described as measured visibility.
- “Independent evaluations,” “live AI rankings,” “automatic publishing,” or “verified lift” until those systems are working and the displayed result has supporting evidence.
- Numeric customer outcomes, engine coverage, savings, or market-share claims inferred from sample data.
